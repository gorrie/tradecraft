# The only Axis-3 result did not survive its own docket check

**2026-09-01, and this supersedes the version of this file written two hours earlier.**

## What was claimed

Telemedicine flexibilities: 12 documents, 8 forward movements, 0 back, asymmetry +1.000,
**p = 0.0078, RESOLVABLE.** Quoted repeatedly as the project's first and only direct
measurement of Axis-3 reversibility, and as evidence the method discriminates.

## What is true

**Docket No. DEA-407. Seven documents. Four forward, zero back. p = 0.125. NOT RESOLVABLE.**

The series itself is real and reads exactly as a ratchet should: Temporary Extension, then
Second, then Third, then Fourth, on one docket, over two years and ten months. A reader looking
at it sees a measure extended four times and never withdrawn.

Four one-way movements is also what four coin flips landing the same way looks like. The
detector's own bar — five directional movements at p < 0.05 — refuses it, and that refusal is
correct.

## Why the first number was wrong

Series membership was decided by looking for the measure's words in a document title. That
admitted `Expansion of Buprenorphine Treatment via Telemedicine Encounter`, which carries
**Docket No. DEA-948** and belongs to a different rulemaking. Three such documents joined the
count and took the forward total from 4 to 8, and 8 clears the bar where 4 does not.

Both settings of the title filter were wrong, in opposite directions, and both were measured
wrong the same afternoon:

| filter | failure |
|---|---|
| ANY distinctive word | admitted *"Medicare Program; FY 2027 Hospice Wage Index"* into the series for "suspicious activity report requirement" — both contain "requirement". 23 documents, all strangers. |
| ALL distinctive words | deleted the entire 83-document "continued dumping duty" series, and cut telemedicine to 4. |

Neither is a repair for the other. Title text was never the right key, and the Federal Register
had published the right one on every document the whole time: `docket_ids`. Membership is now a
published identifier, and every result prints the docket it used.

## The sweep, re-run correctly

Thirteen measures, list fixed before any of it ran. **Nothing resolves.** Four measures show
directional movement; none reaches five movements at p < 0.05.

That is a finding about the method's power rather than about administrative process. At the
document counts a single federal docket generates over a few years, a one-way run is rarely
long enough to distinguish from chance by this test. The ratchet is visible to a reader and not
certifiable by the instrument, which is an uncomfortable and honest place to stop.

## What this costs and what it leaves

Costs: the project has **no measured Axis-3 result.** The claim that the method discriminates
also weakens — it rested on telemedicine at +1.000 against continued dumping duty at −1.000,
and the second of those has not been re-measured under docket identity.

Leaves: a detector that refuses to certify its own headline, a membership rule that is a
published identifier rather than a guess, and a defect found by asking a question that should
have been asked before the first number was ever quoted — *which documents are actually in
this series?*

## What would settle it

More movements per series, which means either longer series or a classifier that reads body
text rather than titles. `movements.yaml` reads titles because titles are cheap and auditable;
five of thirteen measures produced zero directional movement under it, including the
surveillance and identity surfaces this series is about. Body-text classification is a
different instrument with a different floor and it is the honest next step, not a patch.
